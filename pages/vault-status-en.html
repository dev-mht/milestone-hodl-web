$MHT V2 — Status report

The $MHT V2 vault
is frozen. Here is why.

The milestone mechanism can no longer fire, and it never could. Below: what broke, whose fault it is, what still works, and how to check every number yourself without taking our word for it.

Published 4 September 2026  ·  All figures read on-chain the same day
01 — What we found

761,706,453 MHT — 76.17% of the total supply — sit inside the $MHT V2 contract and can never come out.

Not stolen. Not moved. Not held by anyone. Frozen inside the contract, permanently, including the 500,000,000 of the Smart-Vault that was meant to be distributed to holders milestone after milestone.

No milestone has ever fired: milestonesReached returns 0, and rewardsPool returns 0. Not because the market cap never got there — because the function that releases them cannot succeed. It never could.

The mechanism was broken from day one. We did not see it.
02 — The cause: our own configuration mistake

The vault was released by performUpkeep(), a function Chainlink Automation was supposed to call. That function starts with one guard: it checks that the caller is the address stored in automationRegistry.

The address we registered is the Chainlink Registry. But in Chainlink Automation, the actual caller of performUpkeep() is never the Registry — it is the upkeep's Forwarder, a different contract with a different address.

So the guard compared two addresses that could never match. Every call was rejected. _executeMilestone() never ran, rewardsPool was never funded, and not one token ever left the vault.

Read it yourself

Registered in the contract: 0xDc21E279934fF6721CaDfDD112DAfb3261f09A2C
Forwarder of our upkeep: 0x4256…7Fb0

Two different addresses. That is the whole bug. One line of configuration, chosen when the contract was deployed, and never tested against a real milestone because the market cap had not reached the first one yet.

This is our mistake, not Chainlink's. Their documentation is correct. We read it wrong, and we deployed before proving the path end to end.
03 — The second cause: Chainlink discontinued the service

While we were looking at something else, Chainlink announced it was moving its product line to CRE and discontinuing the Automation service our contract depended on. We cancelled the upkeep and withdrew the remaining LINK on 20 August 2026.

So there were two independent causes, and either one alone was fatal. Our configuration mistake meant performUpkeep() could never succeed, even with a Chainlink service in perfect working order. And the discontinuation meant that even a correctly configured contract would have stopped being triggered.

Chronologically, it is that migration that made us reopen the file — and that is how we found our own error, which had gone unnoticed until then.

We are not putting this on a third party. Chainlink evolved its offering, which is its right. Our contract was already broken before that, by our own hand.

04 — Why it cannot be repaired

The contract has a function to fix exactly this: setAutomationRegistry(). It is onlyOwner.

And $MHT V2 renounced its ownership on 5 May 2026. owner() returns the zero address. There is no admin key, no multisig, no backdoor — which is precisely what we promised, and precisely what makes this permanent.

The property that protects holders from a founder emptying the vault is the same property that stops anyone, including us, from repairing this. We would not undo that choice. But it has a price, and this page is that price.

06 — What happens for holders

A version 3 of the $MHT contract is in preparation. It is written on the principle we should have applied from the start: no dependency on Chainlink, or on any third party, for the trigger.

A migration bonus is planned for $MHT V2 holders. Its terms — ratio, snapshot date, mechanics — will be published before the migration, on this site. We are not announcing numbers we have not finalised, and we are not asking anyone to act on this page.

There is no deployment date to announce yet. When there is one, it will be posted here, on X and on Telegram, and nowhere else.

Nothing on this page is a promise of value, of price, or of return. It describes a technical failure and the mechanism we intend to publish to answer it. Holding digital assets carries a risk of total loss.
07 — What we changed on $RMHT

$RMHT, launched on Robinhood Chain on 4 September 2026, was designed against this exact failure. The same choices will carry into $MHT V3.

  • No third party in the trigger. Milestones are fired by pokeMilestone(), a public function anyone can call. No keeper, no subscription to fund, no service that can be discontinued.
  • Price read from the pool itself. A 30-minute time-weighted average read directly on-chain, fail-closed: if the data is not there, the contract does nothing rather than acting on a wrong number.
  • Three confirmations, ten minutes apart, inside a two-hour window before a milestone is accepted. A wick does not release a vault.
  • Residual powers written down, not hidden. What remains after the renounce is documented publicly, including what it could do wrong.
